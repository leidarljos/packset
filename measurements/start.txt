Each pack is written by the test: claims of about 8 KiB as JSON, each with a 384-d embedding, round-robin over eight workspaces, as a writer from before `updated` left them.
The first start stamps every claim; the second opens the same pack, now current.
Each start runs in a child process; base is its VmRSS before the open, after a start over a one-claim pack, still open, has made the code resident.

Before, the pass read the pack through the map and kept every page it read, so the peak was the pack and about 7 MiB more, nearly all of it file pages.
Now a step ends after 1 MiB of records read, stamped or not, and drops the pages it read from the map before the next step reads.
On these 4 KiB pages the peak is 14 to 16 MiB from 79 MiB to 1108 MiB of pack, and the pages read are gone once the pass ends.
The claims of eight workspaces are interleaved in the file, so a step over one workspace's claims faults in every eighth claim, and the kernel maps the 16 pages around each fault (`fault_around_bytes` 65536).
A step therefore maps about eight times what it reads before it drops them, which is most of the 8 to 10 MiB over base.
Over one workspace, `store::tests::a_start_holds_one_step_not_the_pack`, as `cargo test` builds it, writes a pack four times a bound it derives from the host and the pack's tree, and asserts both starts peak under it.

The kernel does not say which filesystems hold large folios, so on ext4 the bound takes the 2 MiB folio; on this kernel a start over ext4 peaks as one over a plain tmpfs, where a fault maps 64 KiB.
On 177c9f5333e2613b17f6481a95c7c5c5ac321600 the same test peaked at more than the pack on each and failed.
The pages a step drops are faulted in again when a later step reads them, so by the medians the pass took 8 to 9% longer on a first start, and a third to a half longer on a current pack: 80 to 106 ms at 79 MiB, 821 to 1214 ms at 791 MiB.
It runs behind the listener; the open is 0.1 ms at every size, before and after.
At 140k claims the 1108 MiB in use is past develop's 1 GiB map, and LMDB then opens the map at the pages in use (`mdb_env_open2` in `mdb.c`), which leaves no room to write.
Every start's pass stopped with MDB_MAP_FULL and left claims unstamped; its time includes the count of what was left.
After, the map opened at twice the data file and the pass stamped every claim.
The first start stays open in each child: closing it freed LMDB's 2 MiB list of dirty pages, glibc moved its mmap threshold up to that size, and the next open cleared its list on the heap, which charged 2048 KiB and about 0.7 ms to the open measured.
