What a start costs as the pack grows, and what the start test checks.
`Service::open` runs before packsetd binds, and the `stamp` pass runs behind the listener, one after the other in one process.

## The start test

`store::tests::a_start_holds_one_step_not_the_pack` writes a pack of one workspace, starts the writer over it in a child process twice, and asserts that each start peaks under a bound.
The first start runs the `stamp` pass over every claim, and the second opens the same pack, now current.
The test runs as `cargo test` builds it, unoptimised:

```console
$ TMPDIR=<dir on the filesystem> cargo test -p packset-daemon --lib store::tests::a_start_holds_one_step_not_the_pack -- --exact --nocapture
```

Each filesystem ran it six times with the store before the fix, six times after it, and six times after it with `measurements/start-moqq/hold-21-mib.patch` applied.
With that patch the pass keeps 21 MiB of heap.
A pass that kept 20 steps of 1048 KiB would keep as much.
`measurements/start-moqq/run.sh` ran every condition, each in a worktree of its own, and `measurements/start-moqq/summary.py` gives the medians and interquartile ranges below:

```console
$ sudo mkdir -m 1777 /mnt/hugefs/tmp /mnt/plainfs/tmp
$ measurements/start-moqq/before.sh 177c9f5333e2613b17f6481a95c7c5c5ac321600 /tmp/wt-before
$ for v in after hold-21-mib old-harness old-warm-up no-mallopt; do git worktree add --detach /tmp/wt-$v da0de82c9a22ddbe0f05045a08da956332781d14; done
$ for v in hold-21-mib old-harness old-warm-up no-mallopt; do git -C /tmp/wt-$v apply "$PWD/measurements/start-moqq/$v.patch"; done
$ measurements/start-moqq/run.sh measurements/start-moqq/conditions.txt 6
$ python3 measurements/start-moqq/summary.py
```

The quartiles are `statistics.quantiles(n=4, method="inclusive")`.

### The fault unit

The test measures what one fault maps of the pack, on the filesystem the pack is on, before each start.
Each probe maps the data file afresh, reads one byte, and takes that mapping's fields from `/proc/self/smaps`.
The next probe reads the byte past what the last one mapped, so the probes step through the whole file.
The unit is the most that any probe mapped.

| pack on | probes | Rss KiB | FilePmdMapped KiB | AnonHugePages KiB | KernelPageSize KiB | MMUPageSize KiB |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ext4 | 92 before the stamping start, 126 before the current one | 64 | 0 | 0 | 4 | 4 |
| tmpfs | 92 and 126 | 64 | 0 | 0 | 4 | 4 |
| tmpfs, `huge=always` | 48 and 50 | 2048 | 0 | 0 | 4 | 4 |

On ext4 a fault maps the 64 KiB of `fault_around_bytes`, as it does on a plain tmpfs.
On a tmpfs mounted `huge=always` one fault brings in the whole 2 MiB folio, with 4 KiB page table entries rather than one page middle directory (PMD) entry.
Before this change the bound also took 2 MiB on ext4.
The reason is that the kernel does not say which filesystems use large folios.
That gave 12 places of 2052 KiB each, 24624 KiB of a 26848 KiB bound, where this kernel maps 68 KiB a place.

### The bound

The bound is 2 steps, plus one fault unit and one page for each place a step reads, plus glibc's 128 KiB trim threshold.
A step is 131 claims of 2 overflow pages each, which is 1048 KiB.
On a tree of depth 2 with 66 or 70 claims a leaf, a step reads 9 places: the meta page, 2 levels, 2 more leaves with the level above them, 2 run ends and 2 free-list pages.
So the bound is 2096 + 9 * (64 + 4) + 128 = 2836 KiB with a 64 KiB unit, and 2096 + 9 * (2048 + 4) + 128 = 20692 KiB with a 2048 KiB unit.

### The smallest regression the bound catches

A start fails when what it keeps beyond a correct start is more than the headroom.
The headroom is the bound less the correct start's peak.

| pack on | start | headroom KiB, median (IQR) | largest headroom KiB | in steps of 1048 KiB |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ext4 | stamping | 600 (600 to 645) | 664 | 0.6 |
| ext4 | current | 1560 (1560 to 1608) | 1624 | 1.5 |
| tmpfs | stamping | 424 (376 to 472) | 488 | 0.5 |
| tmpfs | current | 1648 (1600 to 1648) | 1688 | 1.6 |
| tmpfs, `huge=always` | stamping | 5496 (5496 to 5544) | 5560 | 5.3 |
| tmpfs, `huge=always` | current | 6416 (6384 to 6448) | 6512 | 6.2 |

On ext4, a start that keeps 1624 KiB more than a correct one fails in every run, and on plain tmpfs one that keeps 1688 KiB more.
Two steps more is past both.
On a tmpfs mounted `huge=always` the same goes for 6512 KiB or seven steps more.
The 21 MiB mutation fails on all three filesystems: 19.4 to 20.6 MiB past the bound on ext4 and plain tmpfs, and 14.7 to 15.7 MiB past it on `huge=always`.

### Why the pack is twice its bound

The pack has to be as large as what a correct start keeps plus the smallest regression past it, so that a pass has that much to keep.
A correct start peaks above its base and under its bound.
So its headroom is less than one bound.
A pack of twice the bound is therefore large enough on any host, and a pass that keeps what it reads then ends more than a headroom past the bound.
Here the largest headroom for its bound was 1688 of 2836 KiB, on plain tmpfs, 0.60 of it, so 1.60 bounds would have done on this host.

### What a start leaves after the pass

The start child reads `RssAnon`, `RssFile` and `RssShmem` from `/proc/self/status` at the base and after the pass, and the `Rss` of every mapping from `/proc/self/smaps`.
`VmRSS` and `VmHWM` are per-CPU counters that the kernel reads without each CPU's pending counts (`get_mm_counter` in include/linux/mm.h), while the `smaps` total counts each mapped page.
On ext4, with the store after the fix, six runs of each variant left this much over the base:

A cell without a range had an interquartile range of zero.
With the start child as it was at 6dcab27, a `stamping` start left 1176 KiB over its base, and 1172 KiB of it was `RssAnon`.
Less the 128 KiB the test binary grew by, that is 1048 KiB, inside the 1016 to 1612 KiB that the CI logs for 6dcab27 show; CI was not rerun for this file.
Pinning glibc's trim threshold with `mallopt` takes the `RssAnon` to 176 KiB, so about 1 MiB of it came with glibc's dynamic thresholds.
Setting `M_TRIM_THRESHOLD` turns off the dynamic mmap threshold too (mallopt(3)), so the start child now sets it to the 128 KiB that the bound counts.
This file does not identify which freed chunk moved the threshold, or which allocation then took that 1 MiB from the arena.
Warming up over a copy of the pack takes the remaining `RssAnon` from 176 KiB to 48 KiB.
So 128 KiB was allocated the first time a start ran over a pack this size, which a one-claim warm start did not do.
Without the `mallopt` call, the warm starts close their environments, and the 2 MiB list of dirty pages that Lightning Memory-Mapped Database (LMDB) frees then moves glibc's mmap threshold to that size.
The next open takes its list from the heap and clears it.
That is the 2040 KiB in that row, and the test fails.
With this test, nothing yet accounts for 16 to 148 KiB of `RssAnon` after a `stamping` start on the three filesystems, or for 8 to 12 KiB after a current one.

### Warm-up

The old warm-up was one start over a one-claim pack, kept open.
In the two variants that use it, the test binary grew inside every measured start, by 64 to 128 KiB, and by 128 KiB in 19 of the 24.
In CI at 6dcab27, one `stamping` start grew it by 2868 KiB.
The start child now runs warm starts through the same function that measures the start, each over a copy of the pack beside it on the same filesystem.
Those warm starts end when the binary's resident pages stop growing, or one start past the binary's page count.
That took 2 warm starts before each of the 120 measured starts here that warm up this way.
The test fails if the measured start then grows the binary, and in those 120 starts it grew by 0 KiB.
The peak is reset through `/proc/self/clear_refs` at the base, so the warm starts do not count in `VmHWM`.

### macOS

The start test reads `/proc/self/status`, `/proc/self/smaps`, `/proc/self/mountinfo` and sysfs, which macOS does not have.
On macOS it is ignored, and `cargo test` prints the reason beside it.
These runs were all on Linux, and the macOS build was not run for this file.

Before the fix, the pass read the pack through the map and kept every page it read.
The peak was the pack and about 7 MiB more, nearly all of it file pages.
Now a step ends after reading 1 MiB of records, whether they needed a `stamp` or not, and drops the pages it read from the map before the next step reads.
On these 4 KiB pages the peak is 8954 to 10086 KiB over the base, from 79 MiB to 1108 MiB of pack, and the pages read are gone once the pass ends.
The claims of eight workspaces are interleaved in the file, so a step over one workspace's claims faults in every eighth claim.
The kernel maps the 16 pages around each fault, the 64 KiB measured above.
So a step maps about eight times what it reads before it drops them.
That is most of the peak over the base.
The pages a step drops are faulted in again when a later step reads them.

It runs behind the listener, and the open is 0.1 ms at every size, before and after.
At 140k claims the 1108 MiB in use is past the 1 GiB map that 177c9f5 opened, and Lightning Memory-Mapped Database (LMDB) then opens the map at the pages in use (`mdb_env_open2` in `mdb.c`), which leaves no room to write.
Before the fix, every start's pass stopped with MDB_MAP_FULL and left claims unstamped.
Its time includes the count of what was left.
After the fix, the map opened at twice the data file, and the pass left no claim unstamped.
